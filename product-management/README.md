# Product Management

The product management ladder, from intern to Chief Product Officer. Each level folder
contains the topics we expect the candidate to know (`categories/`), the questions we
actually ask (`questions/`), and the bar we hold them to (`rubric.md`).

## The ladder

| Folder | Title | Scope | Autonomy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [l1-intern](./l1-intern) | Product Intern | A task, a research question, one small feature slice | Works from a defined brief, checked daily |
| [l2-junior](./l2-junior) | Associate Product Manager (APM) | A feature or a small surface | Owns the backlog, escalates decisions |
| [l3-mid](./l3-mid) | Product Manager | A product area with its own metric | Owns the roadmap for the area |
| [l4-senior](./l4-senior) | Senior Product Manager | A complex area or a core business metric | Sets direction, negotiates dependencies |
| [l5-lead](./l5-lead) | Lead / Principal / Group PM | A portfolio, or the hardest problem in the org | Sets strategy, grows other PMs |
| [l6-head](./l6-head) | Head of Product / Director / VP | The product org for a business line | Owns the operating model and the team |
| [l7-c-level](./l7-c-level) | Chief Product Officer (CPO) | Product across the company | Accountable to the CEO and the board |

## What changes as you go up

* **L1–L2** — assessed on fundamentals and potential. Can they think clearly about users and write clearly about problems?
* **L3–L4** — assessed on judgement and delivery. Do they ship the right thing, and can they show why it was right?
* **L5** — assessed on leverage. Do other people get better because this person is in the room?
* **L6–L7** — assessed on the system. Strategy, org design, operating cadence, and the quality of the leaders they hire.
