# Rubric — L7 Chief Product Officer

The CPO is accountable for product across the company, to the CEO and the board. They
are assessed on **judgement at company scale, the leaders they build, and the truth they
are willing to tell**. Delivery stories are context here, not evidence.

## Competencies and the bar

| Competency | Below level (1–2) | At level (3) | Above level (4) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Company strategy** | Business-line strategy scaled up | Coherent company product strategy with real sacrifice and sequencing | Identified a structural shift early and repositioned the company on it |
| **Org & talent** | Has run a large org | Hires and develops VPs; has a bench and a succession plan | Alumni run product organisations elsewhere; org outlives them |
| **Business & P&L** | Fluent in metrics | Accountable for company product economics; forecasts and hits | Changed the company's business model or margin structure |
| **Board & exec** | Presents well, avoids conflict | Trusted by CEO and board, including with bad news | Shifts board-level thinking; peers seek their judgement |
| **Operating system** | Imports a framework | Designs how product decisions are made across the company | Left behind a culture that keeps working after they leave |
| **Integrity under pressure** | Optimistic reporting | Tells the truth early, with a plan | Has taken a costly principled position and can prove it |

## Must have

* Has hired, developed and exited executives.
* Has been accountable for a company-level number and can state it.
* Has led through at least one genuine crisis.
* Can describe a strategy they were wrong about, and what it cost.
* References that include a CEO, a board member, and a former direct report.

## Nice to have

* Experience through a stage transition similar to ours (e.g. scale-up to public, or regional to global).
* Board experience in another company.
* Domain credibility with our customers and market.

## Red flags

* Strategy with no sacrifice and no explicit bet.
* Cannot name a leader they built.
* Every failure is attributed to the CEO, the board, or the market.
* Avoids specifics about numbers they owned.
* Talks about culture entirely in values-poster language with no behavioural example.

## Reference checks (mandatory at this level)

Ask a former CEO: "Would you hire them again, and for what?"
Ask a former direct report: "What did they do when you failed?"
Ask a peer executive: "Where did they cost the company something?"

## First 180 days if hired

Publish a written diagnosis of the product organisation and strategy, make the
leadership calls that are already clear, establish the product operating system, and
commit to one company-level number.
