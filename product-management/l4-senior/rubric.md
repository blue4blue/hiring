# Rubric — L4 Senior Product Manager

A senior PM owns a complex area or a core business metric. They are assessed on
**judgement under ambiguity and influence without authority**. The step from L3 is not
more delivery — it is setting direction others follow.

## Competencies and the bar

| Competency | Below level (1–2) | At level (3) | Above level (4) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Strategy** | Roadmap dressed as strategy | Names a diagnosis, a policy and what they will not do | Reframes the problem in a way that changes the org's plan |
| **Discovery** | Discovery equals user interviews | Runs a portfolio of cheap tests; de-risks unfalsifiable bets | Builds a discovery system the whole area runs on |
| **Execution at scale** | Struggles beyond one squad | Runs a multi-team programme with real milestones and rollback plans | Anticipates the failure mode before anyone else names it |
| **Experimentation** | Reads tests correctly, nothing more | Designs the programme; handles interference and long-term effects | Chooses the right causal method when randomisation is impossible |
| **Influence** | Escalates every disagreement | Aligns skeptical peers and executives with a written case | Changes senior minds and leaves the relationship stronger |
| **Business acumen** | Cannot connect product to revenue | Understands unit economics and the levers they control | Makes commercial trade-offs an exec would make |

## Must have

* Has owned a business-relevant metric, not just a feature metric.
* Has changed a senior stakeholder's mind and can describe how.
* Has run something multi-team and multi-quarter.
* Has been meaningfully wrong and can tell that story without defensiveness.

## Nice to have

* Mentoring or informal leadership of other PMs.
* Domain expertise in our market.
* Experience with pricing, packaging or monetisation.

## Red flags

* Strategy talk with no diagnosis and no sacrifice.
* All influence is escalation.
* Cannot name a metric that got worse because of a choice they made.
* Describes their scale purely by team size rather than by the problem's difficulty.

## First 90 days if hired

Publish a written area strategy, establish the metric tree and the discovery cadence,
and land one cross-team decision that was previously stuck.
