# Product Analytics

The product analytics ladder, from intern to Chief Data / Analytics Officer. Covers
product analysts and the analytics leadership above them — the people who decide what
gets measured, whether a result is real, and what the business should do about it.

## The ladder

| Folder | Title | Scope | Autonomy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [l1-intern](./l1-intern) | Analytics Intern | A defined query or a small analysis | Works from a specified question |
| [l2-junior](./l2-junior) | Junior Product Analyst | A feature or a dashboard | Answers scoped questions; escalates interpretation |
| [l3-mid](./l3-mid) | Product Analyst | A product area's measurement | Owns metrics, tests and analysis for the area |
| [l4-senior](./l4-senior) | Senior Product Analyst | A business-critical area or a core metric | Chooses the method; challenges the question itself |
| [l5-lead](./l5-lead) | Lead / Principal Analyst | Analytics across a portfolio; the experimentation bar | Sets standards and grows analysts |
| [l6-head](./l6-head) | Head of Product Analytics | The analytics org for a business line | Owns team, platform, governance and business partnership |
| [l7-c-level](./l7-c-level) | Chief Data / Analytics Officer | Data across the company | Accountable for data strategy, trust and risk |

## Assessing analysts well

* **Always run a hands-on SQL round** from L2 up. Take-home or live, but real. Reading someone's SQL tells you more than an hour of discussion.
* **Give them a messy question, not a clean one.** "Why did retention drop?" is the job. "Write a query that returns X" is not.
* **Test whether they push back.** The best analysts refuse the question as asked and reframe it.
* **Check statistical honesty.** Ask about a result that was significant but wrong. Anyone who has never had one has not looked.
* **Do not confuse tool knowledge with analytical judgement.** Tools are learnable in weeks; judgement is not.
