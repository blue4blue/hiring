# Rubric — L3 Product Analyst

A product analyst owns measurement for a product area: definitions, tests, dashboards
and the analyses that change decisions. Assessed on **judgement plus technical depth**.

## Competencies and the bar

| Competency | Below level (1–2) | At level (3) | Above level (4) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **SQL & modelling** | Writes queries, does not shape models | Designs tested, performant models others build on | Improves the warehouse architecture and reduces cost meaningfully |
| **Metrics** | Uses inherited definitions | Designs a framework; governs instrumentation; spots gameability | Redefines a metric that was quietly misleading the company |
| **Experimentation** | Runs standard tests | Designs tests properly; validity checks; variance reduction | Chooses the right method when a clean A/B test is impossible |
| **Analysis** | Answers as asked | Reframes the question; finds the real driver; sizes opportunities | Produces analysis that changes the roadmap |
| **Communication** | Reports numbers | Leads with the decision; honest about uncertainty | Persuades a skeptical executive with evidence |

## Must have

* Strong live SQL including window functions and time logic.
* Has designed and analysed real experiments, including a failed one.
* Has caught a data quality problem that mattered.
* Has changed a decision with an analysis.

## Nice to have

* dbt / analytics engineering.
* Python for modelling beyond pandas basics.
* Causal inference exposure.

## Red flags

* Talks about experimentation entirely in terms of a tool's UI.
* Never questions the question.
* No example of being wrong.
* Cannot explain what a p-value does not mean.

## First 90 days if hired

Own the area's metric framework, audit instrumentation, run at least two experiments
end to end, and publish one analysis that changes the roadmap.
