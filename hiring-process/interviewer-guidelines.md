# Interviewer Guidelines

## Running the interview

* Introduce yourself, your role, and the plan for the session in the first two minutes.
* Aim for a 20/80 split: you talk 20% of the time.
* Ask one question, then be quiet. Silence is where the real answer lives.
* Follow up on the specific rather than the general: "Which metric?", "Who disagreed?", "What did you do next?"
* Always leave 5 minutes for the candidate's questions. What they ask is signal.

## Probing well

Every strong answer survives three levels of "why". Use these:

* "Walk me through the actual sequence — what happened first?"
* "What were the other options, and why did you rule them out?"
* "What would have changed your mind?"
* "What did you get wrong, and when did you notice?"
* "Who disagreed with you, and how did it end?"

## Structure of a good behavioural answer (STAR)

* **Situation** — context and constraints.
* **Task** — what they specifically owned.
* **Action** — what they personally did.
* **Result** — the outcome, with a number where one exists.

If a candidate keeps answering in the abstract ("generally I would…"), pull them back to
one concrete instance.

## What not to ask

Do not ask about age, marital or family status, pregnancy or plans to have children,
religion, ethnicity or nationality beyond right-to-work, health or disability, military
service where not legally relevant, or salary history where it is restricted. If a
candidate volunteers any of it, do not record it and do not weigh it.

## Assessing at the right level

* An intern is assessed on **potential and fundamentals**, not on outcomes.
* A mid-level candidate is assessed on **reliable ownership of a scoped problem**.
* A senior candidate is assessed on **judgement under ambiguity and influence without authority**.
* A leader is assessed on **the quality of the system and people they leave behind**.

Never punish a junior candidate for lacking strategic scope, and never accept a leader
who can only describe their own individual output.
