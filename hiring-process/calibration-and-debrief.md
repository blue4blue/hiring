# Calibration and Debrief

## Before the loop starts

* Agree on the level you are hiring for and open the matching `rubric.md`. If two people
  on the panel disagree about the level, stop and resolve that first — you cannot
  calibrate against a target you have not defined.
* Assign one competency to each interviewer and write it in the invite.
* Agree what "must have" means for this specific role. Everything else is "nice to have"
  and cannot block a hire.

## The debrief

1. **Submit first, discuss second.** Anyone who has not written their scorecard does not speak first.
2. Go round the table with the recommendation and the single strongest piece of evidence behind it.
3. Discuss disagreements by evidence, not by seniority. "What did you see that I did not?"
4. Decide. Hire, no hire, or hire at a different level. "Let's do one more round" is a decision to say no unless there is a specific, nameable gap that a specific extra round would close.

## Calibration habits

* Review your last ten hires against their scorecards twice a year. Which questions predicted performance? Drop the ones that did not.
* Track pass-through rates per interviewer. An interviewer who passes everyone, or no one, is not calibrating.
* Shadow before you solo. Every new interviewer sits in on two loops before running one.
* Re-read the rubric before every interview. Level drift is the most common cause of a bad product hire.

## Common failure modes

* **Level drift.** Interviewing a mid candidate against a senior rubric, or the reverse. Fix by naming the level out loud at the start of the debrief.
* **Halo from pedigree.** A well-known former employer is context, not evidence.
* **Similarity bias.** "I would grab a coffee with them" is not a competency. Replace "culture fit" with a named value and evidence for it.
* **Consensus decay.** The loudest voice in the debrief anchors the room. Written-first scorecards exist to prevent this.
* **Over-indexing on the case.** A case is a two-hour artificial exercise. Weight it accordingly against years of shipped work.
