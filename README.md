<h1 align="center">
    Product Hiring
</h1>

<p align="center">
    Interview guides and hiring rubrics for product roles — from intern to C-level.
</p>

</br>

---

## What this is

A structured hiring guide for the five product-family disciplines. It is organised the
same way an engineering interview repo is: by **position type** first, then by **level**,
and inside each level by **topic**.

Every level folder contains three things:

* **`categories/`** — the topics we expect a candidate at this level to know, with links to further reading. Use these to plan an interview, or send them to a candidate as a study guide.
* **`questions/`** — the questions we actually ask, with follow-ups underneath designed to test depth rather than vocabulary.
* **`rubric.md`** — the bar. What "below level", "at level" and "above level" look like for each competency, what is must-have versus nice-to-have, the red flags, and what the first 90 days should produce.

## How to use it

1. **Pick the level honestly first.** Most bad product hires are level-drift: interviewing a mid candidate against a senior rubric, or the reverse. Open `rubric.md` before you open `questions/`.
2. **Assign one competency per interviewer** and put it in the calendar invite. Two people testing the same thing is a wasted round.
3. **Take the questions as starting points.** The follow-ups matter more than the questions — they are where you find out whether the candidate did the work or watched someone else do it.
4. **Write the scorecard before the debrief.** Use [hiring-process/scorecard-template.md](./hiring-process/scorecard-template.md).
5. **Adapt to your context.** Level names differ by company; the ladder table in each family's README maps them to scope and autonomy, which is what actually matters.

## The levels

| Level | Typical title | The question being answered |
| --- | --- | --- |
| L1 | Intern | Do they have the fundamentals and the curiosity? |
| L2 | Junior / Associate | Can they own a scoped piece of work reliably? |
| L3 | Mid | Do they exercise judgement, not just execution? |
| L4 | Senior | Can they set direction under ambiguity and influence without authority? |
| L5 | Lead / Principal | Does everyone around them get better? |
| L6 | Head / Director / VP | Can they build the org and the system? |
| L7 | C-level | Can they be trusted with company-scale judgement, and will they tell the truth? |
