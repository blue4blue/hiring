# Product Operations

Product Ops makes the product organisation work: the cadence, the tooling, the data
that reaches decision-makers, the customer signal that reaches teams, and the launch
machinery. It is a young discipline, so title inflation and role confusion are common —
be explicit about which of the three flavours you are hiring for.

## The three flavours of Product Ops

Most Product Ops roles are a mix of these, but one usually dominates. Decide which
before you write the job description, and interview for that one.

1. **Process and cadence** — planning, rituals, roadmap governance, goal-setting, launch readiness.
2. **Data and insight** — instrumentation hygiene, reporting, voice of customer, competitive and market feeds.
3. **Tooling and systems** — the product stack (roadmapping, feedback, analytics, experimentation), integrations and administration.

## The ladder

| Folder | Title | Scope | Autonomy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [l1-intern](./l1-intern) | Product Ops Intern | A task or a report | Works from a defined brief |
| [l2-junior](./l2-junior) | Product Operations Associate | One process or one tool | Runs it; escalates changes |
| [l3-mid](./l3-mid) | Product Operations Manager | A product group's operating cadence | Owns cadence, tooling and reporting for the group |
| [l4-senior](./l4-senior) | Senior Product Operations Manager | Cross-team processes and programmes | Designs process; runs cross-functional programmes |
| [l5-lead](./l5-lead) | Lead / Principal Product Ops | The operating model across the org | Sets standards; drives change; mentors |
| [l6-head](./l6-head) | Head of Product Operations | The product operating model and the ops team | Owns team, model, planning and governance |
| [l7-c-level](./l7-c-level) | COO / Chief of Staff to Product | Company operating model | Accountable for execution across the company |

## Assessing Product Ops well

* **Ask what they removed**, not just what they added. Anyone can add process.
* **Look for measured process outcomes** — cycle time, planning duration, decision latency, adoption rates.
* **Test change management.** The job is mostly getting people to work differently, which is a people problem, not a tooling problem.
* **Beware the ceremony administrator.** Running a meeting is not designing an operating model.
