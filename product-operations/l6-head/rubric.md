# Rubric — L6 Head of Product Operations

Owns the product operating model and the ops team for a business line. Assessed on
**whether the organisation demonstrably works better**, measured, not asserted.

## Competencies and the bar

| Competency | Below level (1–2) | At level (3) | Above level (4) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Operating model** | Administers the existing model | Designs and measures the model; adapts it by stage | Transformed organisational throughput with evidence |
| **Org & hiring** | Grows the team | Shapes a small team to the model; hires and levels deliberately | Gave work back to product teams and shrank the ops footprint |
| **Planning & governance** | Runs the cycle | Owns planning, commitment discipline and benefit review | Cut planning cost substantially without losing alignment |
| **Business credibility** | Argues in process terms | Owns budget; frames operating work commercially | Changed executive behaviour, not just team behaviour |
| **Integrity** | Reports what leadership wants | Honest status and honest diagnosis | Refused a damaging demand and kept the relationship |

## Must have

* Measured before/after on at least two operating metrics.
* Has managed a team, not only programmes.
* Has owned budget including the stack.
* Has refused an executive request and can describe the outcome.

## Nice to have

* Experience at multiple company stages.
* Background in product, engineering or analytics rather than only PMO.
* Experience through a reorg or a merger.

## Red flags

* Growth of the ops team is presented as the achievement.
* No operating metrics at all.
* Governance added everywhere, removed nowhere.
* Describes the role as "keeping leadership informed".

## First 90 days if hired

Publish a measured diagnosis of the operating model, agree two changes with product and
engineering leadership, cut the highest-cost low-value ritual, and set the team's scope
deliberately small.
