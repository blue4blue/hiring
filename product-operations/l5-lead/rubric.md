# Rubric — L5 Lead / Principal Product Operations

At L5 the question is whether they can **design and change how an organisation works**,
not run it. Leverage comes from the system and from the people they coach.

## Competencies and the bar

| Competency | Below level (1–2) | At level (3) | Above level (4) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Operating model** | Runs processes well | Designs the model across the org and measures it | Transformed a slow org into a fast one, with numbers |
| **Tooling strategy** | Buys good tools | Stack strategy with TCO, vendor leverage and an exit plan | Consolidated a stack and proved the saving and the adoption |
| **Change management** | Communicates changes | Pilots, builds coalitions, adapts; changes stick | Changed a hostile org's behaviour durably |
| **Influence** | Respected by teams | Peer to product and engineering leadership | Executives rely on their read of how the org really works |
| **Mentoring** | Helps colleagues | Defines the craft and grows others in it | Produced product ops leaders |

## Must have

* A designed operating model with measured before/after.
* A change that survived after they stopped pushing it.
* A cost or vendor decision with real numbers.
* Evidence of saying no to leadership and keeping the relationship.

## Nice to have

* Experience across two very different company stages.
* Analytics or engineering background.
* Programme leadership in a regulated environment.

## Red flags

* Every answer is about tooling.
* No measurement of the operating model at all.
* Change described as "we ran a comms plan".
* Cannot name anyone they developed.

## First 90 days if hired

Publish a measured diagnosis of the operating model, agree the two changes worth making
with product and engineering leadership, and pilot the first one.
