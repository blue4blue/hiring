# Rubric — L7 COO / Chief of Staff to Product (executive operations)

At this level the person owns how the company executes. Titles vary — COO, Chief of
Staff, SVP Business Operations — so name the accountability precisely before the loop.
Assessed on **company-scale execution, the leaders they build, and honesty under
pressure**.

## Competencies and the bar

| Competency | Below level (1–2) | At level (3) | Above level (4) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Operating model** | Runs a large PMO | Owns a company operating model that ties strategy to execution | Measurably increased the company's execution speed |
| **Org design** | Has run reorgs | Partners with the CEO on structure; builds an exec bench | Designed an organisation that outperformed after they left |
| **Governance & risk** | Reports status | Owns portfolio governance, risk and crisis command | Killed a major initiative correctly against political resistance |
| **Change** | Runs comms plans | Leads company-wide change that sticks | Changed a culture, evidenced by behaviour not survey scores |
| **Exec & board** | Presents well | Trusted by CEO and board with unwelcome truth | Board relies on their read of execution risk |
| **Integrity** | Manages the message | Makes bad news travel fast by design | Took a real personal cost for saying what was true |

## Must have

* Company-level operating ownership, not just a programme portfolio.
* Has led through a crisis or a contraction.
* Has hired and exited executives.
* Can show measured improvement in how the company executed.
* References including a CEO, a peer executive, and a former direct report.

## Nice to have

* Experience through a stage transition similar to ours.
* Finance or strategy background alongside operations.
* M&A integration experience.

## Red flags

* Achievement described as the size of the PMO.
* Governance added everywhere; nothing removed.
* Every failure attributed to the CEO or to "the culture".
* Cannot name a leader they built.

## Reference checks (mandatory at this level)

Ask a former CEO: "What did they tell you that nobody else would?"
Ask a peer executive: "Did their processes help you or slow you down?"
Ask a former direct report: "What happened when you brought them bad news?"

## First 180 days if hired

Publish a measured diagnosis of how the company executes, agree the operating model with
the CEO and exec team, remove the two largest sources of drag, and establish honest
status reporting.
