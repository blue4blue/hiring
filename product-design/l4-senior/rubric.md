# Rubric — L4 Senior Product Designer

A senior designer owns a complex area or a core journey and sets direction others
follow. Assessed on **judgement, systems thinking and influence** — craft is assumed.

## Competencies and the bar

| Competency | Below level (1–2) | At level (3) | Above level (4) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Design strategy** | Executes the roadmap well | Sets direction for an area and defends the investment in business terms | Changed what the company believed the product could be |
| **Systems & craft** | Strong on single surfaces | Designs coherent multi-surface, multi-role systems; accessibility by design | Solves a class of problems, not an instance |
| **Research & measurement** | Runs standard usability tests | Designs a validation strategy; partners on experiments; measures UX over time | Chooses the right method under real constraints and is honest about limits |
| **Influence** | Persuades within the team | Changes product decisions; trusted by PM and engineering leads | Shifts executive thinking; other teams adopt their approach |
| **Mentoring** | Reviews work occasionally | Develops designers with specific, actionable feedback | Visibly raised the craft level of the people around them |

## Must have

* At least one system-level piece of work, not just features.
* A design decision they lost, and what they did next.
* Evidence of measuring their work, quantitatively or qualitatively.
* Mentoring experience.

## Nice to have

* Design system ownership.
* Experience designing for AI-driven or highly uncertain systems.
* Brand or marketing-adjacent experience.

## Red flags

* Influence described only as "I convinced them it looked better".
* Never lost an argument.
* Accessibility still framed as compliance.
* No mentoring, no critique leadership, no evidence of raising others.

## First 90 days if hired

Publish a written point of view on the area's experience, set the design direction for
one core journey, and establish a critique and quality bar the team runs without them.
