# Product Design

The product design ladder, from intern to Chief Design Officer. Covers product designers
(UX/UI) and the research and systems work that sits alongside them. Each level folder
contains `categories/`, `questions/` and a `rubric.md`.

## The ladder

| Folder | Title | Scope | Autonomy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [l1-intern](./l1-intern) | Design Intern | A screen, a component, a research session | Works from a brief, reviewed daily |
| [l2-junior](./l2-junior) | Junior Product Designer | A feature or flow | Executes with direction; craft still being formed |
| [l3-mid](./l3-mid) | Product Designer | A product area | Owns end-to-end design for the area |
| [l4-senior](./l4-senior) | Senior Product Designer | A complex area or a core journey | Sets design direction; partners as an equal with PM and eng |
| [l5-lead](./l5-lead) | Lead / Staff Product Designer | A portfolio, or the design system | Raises craft across teams; mentors designers |
| [l6-head](./l6-head) | Head of Design / Design Director | The design org for a business line | Owns hiring, process, quality bar and design strategy |
| [l7-c-level](./l7-c-level) | Chief Design Officer (CDO) | Design and brand across the company | Accountable to the CEO for the experience the company delivers |

## Portfolio review — the core of every design interview

At every level from L2 up, the portfolio review is the highest-signal round. Rules:

* Ask for **two or three projects in depth**, not a highlight reel of twelve.
* For each: what was the problem, what constraints existed, what did they try and reject, what shipped, and what happened after it shipped.
* Push for **their** contribution when the work was a team effort. Ask who did what.
* Ask to see the messy middle — sketches, rejected directions, the version that was wrong.
* A candidate who cannot say what they would change now is not reflecting on their work.
