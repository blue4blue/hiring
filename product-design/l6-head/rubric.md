# Rubric — L6 Head of Design / Design Director

A Head of Design owns the design organisation for a business line. Assessed on **the
system they build**: strategy, org, quality bar, and commercial credibility.

## Competencies and the bar

| Competency | Below level (1–2) | At level (3) | Above level (4) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Strategy & vision** | Aggregates team design work | Sets an experience vision with real investment choices | Made experience a durable competitive advantage |
| **Org & hiring** | Fills seats | Shapes the org to the strategy; ladder, calibration and hiring standard | Built a bench; alumni lead design orgs elsewhere |
| **Process & quality** | Adds ceremonies | Designs a workflow that raises quality and reduces friction | Turned a low-maturity org into a high-maturity one, measurably |
| **Business credibility** | Argues aesthetically | Owns budget; argues design in commercial terms; trusted by peers | Changed executive priorities with a design-led argument |
| **Talent judgement** | Avoids hard calls | Manages performance fairly and promptly; grows leaders | Raises the level of every team they touch |

## Must have

* Has managed managers or leads, not only ICs.
* Has owned budget and headcount.
* Has built or fixed a hiring and levelling standard.
* Has held a quality bar under commercial pressure and can name the cost.

## Nice to have

* Research and content design leadership experience, not only visual/product.
* Experience at our stage and the next one.
* Design system governance at scale.

## Red flags

* Describes design's problem as "the company doesn't get design" with no plan.
* No commercial framing anywhere in their answers.
* Never blocked anything, or blocked everything.
* Cannot name a leader they developed.

## First 90 days if hired

Publish a written diagnosis of the experience and the org, fix the hiring and levelling
standard, make the obvious people calls, and set the quality bar in practice — not in a
deck.
