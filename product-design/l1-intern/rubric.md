# Rubric — L1 Design Intern

Assessed on **eye, curiosity and coachability** — not on shipped outcomes. A weak
portfolio with sharp reasoning beats a beautiful portfolio the candidate cannot explain.

## Competencies and the bar

| Competency | Below level (1–2) | At level (3) | Above level (4) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Craft** | Misaligned, inconsistent, no hierarchy | Clean layout, consistent spacing and type, considers contrast | Notices and fixes hierarchy problems in unfamiliar work |
| **Interaction** | Designs only the happy path | Designs error, empty and loading states when prompted | Thinks about edge cases and scale unprompted |
| **Research & collaboration** | Has never watched a user | Has observed at least one session; separates said from did | Turns an observation into a specific design change |
| **Tools & process** | Messy, unnamed layers; no components | Organised Figma file; uses components and auto-layout | Others could pick up the file with no explanation |
| **Coachability** | Defends work when critiqued | Takes critique, iterates, explains the change | Asks for critique early and shows two options |

## Must have

* A portfolio, however small, that they can talk through as problem → attempts → outcome.
* Basic Figma competence.
* Willingness to show the messy middle.
* Awareness that accessibility exists.

## Nice to have

* Any real user testing, even informal.
* Basic HTML/CSS literacy.
* Personal projects, illustration, motion or writing that shows range.

## Red flags

* Portfolio is only final screens with no reasoning.
* Cannot say what they would change now.
* Treats accessibility as a nice-to-have or an afterthought.
* Copies a trend without being able to justify it.

## First 90 days if hired

Ship one small feature end to end, observe at least three usability sessions, and
present work in critique at least four times.
