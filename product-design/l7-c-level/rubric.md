# Rubric — L7 Chief Design Officer

The CDO owns the experience the company delivers, across product, brand and every
touchpoint. Assessed on **company-scale judgement, the leaders they build, and whether
the culture they created outlived them**.

## Competencies and the bar

| Competency | Below level (1–2) | At level (3) | Above level (4) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Experience strategy** | Scaled-up design direction | Coherent company design point of view with real refusals | Made design a structural advantage the market recognises |
| **Org design** | Has run a large org | Hires and develops directors/VPs; bench and succession exist | Alumni run design organisations elsewhere; org outlived them |
| **Business impact** | Argues design qualitatively | Owns budget; ties experience to revenue, cost and risk credibly | Changed the company's economics through experience |
| **Exec & board** | Presents well | Peer to CPO/CTO/CMO; trusted with bad news at board level | Shifts board thinking; sought out for judgement beyond design |
| **Culture & craft** | Sets standards | Builds a critique and craft culture that scales | Left a design operating system still working after departure |
| **Ethics** | Follows policy | Has refused manipulative or harmful design and taken the cost | Made ethics a competitive position, publicly and internally |

## Must have

* Has hired, developed and exited design executives.
* Has owned a company-scale budget and defended it under pressure.
* Has led through a crisis affecting user trust.
* Can name a costly principled refusal.
* References including a CEO, a peer executive, and a former direct report.

## Nice to have

* Brand and marketing design ownership as well as product.
* Experience through a stage transition similar to ours.
* Public voice that helps recruiting.

## Red flags

* Design point of view stated only as values, never as refusals.
* Cannot name a leader they built.
* All failures attributed to the CEO or the market.
* Craft talk with no commercial fluency, or commercial talk with no craft.

## Reference checks (mandatory at this level)

Ask a former CEO: "When did they change your mind, and about what?"
Ask a peer executive: "Where did they cost the company something?"
Ask a former director: "What did they do when you failed?"

## First 180 days if hired

Publish a written diagnosis of the company's experience and design organisation, make
the leadership calls that are already clear, establish how experience decisions get made,
and commit to one company-level outcome.
