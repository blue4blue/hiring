# Rubric — L3 Product Marketing Manager

Owns positioning and go-to-market for a product line. Assessed on **the quality of the
words, the rigour of the research, and measured launch outcomes**.

## Competencies and the bar

| Competency | Below level (1–2) | At level (3) | Above level (4) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Positioning** | Adjectives and feature lists | Real positioning with alternatives, proof and a chosen best-fit customer | Repositioned a product and can show the commercial effect |
| **GTM & launch** | Coordinates a checklist | Owns strategy: segment, motion, channel, message, metric | Changed the motion or channel mix and improved the result |
| **Research** | Relies on sales input | Runs win/loss and competitive programmes; talks to customers | Research that redirected product or investment |
| **Enablement / content** | Produces assets | Builds what sellers use and measures adoption | Moved win rate, ramp time or conversion measurably |
| **Cross-functional** | Executes the PM's brief | Peer to PM; shapes what gets built and how it is priced | Trusted by sales leadership and product equally |

## Must have

* A positioning document they wrote, discussed in specifics.
* Direct customer and win/loss conversations.
* At least one launch with numbers attached.
* Evidence that something they made was actually used.

## Nice to have

* Pricing or packaging involvement.
* Analyst relations experience.
* Both B2B and self-serve exposure — but hire for the motion you have.

## Red flags

* Cannot say who the product is not for.
* Launch stories with no post-launch metric.
* Content produced with no evidence of adoption.
* Competitive knowledge sourced entirely from competitors' websites.

## First 90 days if hired

Rewrite positioning for the line with evidence, run ten win/loss conversations, ship one
launch with a scorecard, and fix the single worst-performing enablement asset.
