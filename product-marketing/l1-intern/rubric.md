# Rubric — L1 Product Marketing Intern

Assessed on **writing, curiosity about customers and clear thinking**. Portfolio matters
more than experience; a strong writing sample beats a long CV.

## Competencies and the bar

| Competency | Below level (1–2) | At level (3) | Above level (4) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Writing** | Vague, jargon-heavy, buried point | Clear, specific, leads with the point; edits themselves | Copy that a professional would ship with minor edits |
| **Messaging** | Lists features | Distinguishes benefit from feature; writes for one segment | Chooses a segment to exclude, deliberately |
| **Market curiosity** | Has not looked at competitors | Can research a market quickly and summarise it | Finds the non-obvious alternative or the real objection |
| **Collaboration** | Defensive about edits | Takes feedback and improves the draft | Reconciles conflicting feedback and proposes a resolution |
| **Reliability** | Misses deadlines quietly | Delivers on time, flags early | Others start trusting them with launch-critical copy |

## Must have

* A writing sample of any kind — blog, essay, university work, newsletter.
* Ability to cut their own writing.
* Curiosity about why people buy things.
* Basic understanding of who our customer might be.

## Nice to have

* Any hands-on experience with a CRM, email tool or CMS.
* SEO or social experience.
* Sales, support or retail experience — it teaches objection handling.

## Red flags

* Writes in buzzwords and cannot say what a phrase means.
* Cannot name a competitor after being told the product.
* Treats marketing as decoration on top of a finished product.

## First 90 days if hired

Own release notes and one content stream, sit in on five customer or sales calls, and
write one launch asset that ships.
