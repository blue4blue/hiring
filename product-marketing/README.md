# Product Marketing

Product marketing sits between the product and the market: positioning, messaging,
launch, pricing input, competitive intelligence and sales enablement. It is the most
inconsistently defined role in the product family, so decide which centre of gravity you
are hiring for before writing the job description.

## The four centres of gravity

Most PMM roles combine these; one usually dominates. Interview for the dominant one.

1. **Positioning and messaging** — the narrative, the category, why anyone should care.
2. **Go-to-market and launch** — orchestrating launches across marketing, sales, support and product.
3. **Sales enablement** — arming a sales team: decks, battlecards, objection handling, training. Heaviest in B2B.
4. **Growth and lifecycle** — acquisition, activation, expansion messaging, often close to growth marketing. Heaviest in self-serve.

## Assessing PMMs well

* **Ask for the actual words.** "I repositioned the product" means nothing; the positioning statement, the headline and the three proof points mean everything.
* **Make them write.** A short live or take-home writing exercise is the highest-signal round in the loop.
* **Check they talked to customers**, not just to sales. PMMs who only hear the internal echo produce generic messaging.
* **Look for numbers.** Pipeline influenced, win rate, adoption, attach rate, time-to-first-value.
* **Beware the launch coordinator.** Running a launch checklist is not the same as deciding what the product means to a market.
