# Introduction Questions

Warm-up questions for the first 10–15 minutes of any product interview, at any level
and in any of the five position families. The goal is not to score the candidate yet —
it is to get them talking, to map their real scope of ownership, and to collect the
threads you will pull on later in the loop.

## Background

* Walk me through your career so far, in about five minutes.
* What is the product you worked on most recently? Who used it, and what problem did it solve for them?
* How was your team structured? Who did you work with day to day?
* What were you personally accountable for, as opposed to what the team was accountable for?
* What was the size of the business/product? (users, revenue, transactions — whatever the candidate measures)
* Which part of that work would you happily do again, and which part would you never repeat?

## Scope and ownership

* What decisions could you make alone? Which ones needed sign-off, and from whom?
* What was your budget or headcount influence, if any?
* Describe the largest thing you shipped. How large was the team behind it?
* Where did the ideas you worked on come from — you, leadership, sales, or users?

## Motivation and fit

* Why are you looking to move?
* What do you know about us, and what made you apply here specifically?
* What kind of product problem do you find most energizing?
* What do you want to be true about your next role that was not true about your last one?
* What would make you turn down an otherwise good offer?

## Self-awareness

* What feedback have you received more than once in your career?
* Tell me about a decision you got wrong. How did you find out, and what did you change?
* What is a skill you are deliberately working on right now?
* Who do you learn from? What have you read, listened to, or used recently that changed how you work?

## Red flags to listen for

* Every success is "I", every failure is "the team" or "leadership".
* Cannot name a single metric for a product they claim to have owned.
* Describes the job entirely as ceremonies and tickets, with no user or business outcome.
* Cannot articulate a trade-off they made — only features they delivered.
* No curiosity about our product, users, or market.
